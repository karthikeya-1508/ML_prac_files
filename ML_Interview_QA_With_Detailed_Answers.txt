Machine Learning Interview Preparation Handbook (Interview-Ready Answers)

This document contains sample answers that you can speak in an
interview.

1.  What is Machine Learning? Answer: “Machine Learning is a subset of
    Artificial Intelligence that enables computers to learn patterns
    from historical data and make predictions or decisions without being
    explicitly programmed.”

2.  What are the types of Machine Learning? Answer: “There are four main
    types:

3.  Supervised Learning – learns from labeled data.

4.  Unsupervised Learning – finds patterns in unlabeled data.

5.  Semi-Supervised Learning – uses a small amount of labeled data with
    a large amount of unlabeled data.

6.  Reinforcement Learning – learns by interacting with an environment
    using rewards and penalties.”

7.  Difference between Classification and Regression? Answer:
    “Classification predicts categorical outputs like Spam or Not Spam,
    whereas Regression predicts continuous values like house prices.”

8.  What is Overfitting? Answer: “Overfitting occurs when a model learns
    the training data too well, including its noise. As a result, it
    performs well on training data but poorly on unseen data.”

9.  How do you reduce Overfitting? Answer: “I can reduce overfitting by
    collecting more data, using cross-validation, applying
    regularization, selecting important features, pruning decision
    trees, tuning hyperparameters, or using ensemble methods.”

10. What is Underfitting? Answer: “Underfitting occurs when the model is
    too simple to capture the underlying patterns in the data, resulting
    in poor performance on both training and testing datasets.”

11. Why do we split data into train and test? Answer: “We split the data
    to evaluate how well the model generalizes to unseen data. The model
    learns from the training set and is evaluated on the testing set.”

12. Why is scaling required? Answer: “Scaling ensures that all features
    have a similar range. Algorithms based on distance, such as SVM,
    KNN, K-Means, and Logistic Regression, perform better after
    scaling.”

13. Why doesn’t Random Forest require scaling? Answer: “Random Forest is
    based on decision trees, which split data using feature thresholds
    rather than distance calculations. Therefore, feature scaling is
    generally unnecessary.”

14. Why does SVM require scaling? Answer: “SVM calculates distances
    between data points to find the optimal separating hyperplane.
    Features with larger values can dominate the distance calculation,
    so scaling is important.”

15. When would you choose SVM over Random Forest? Answer: “I would
    choose SVM when the dataset is small to medium-sized, has
    high-dimensional features, and the classes are well separated. For
    example, text classification and gene expression datasets.”

16. When would you choose Random Forest? Answer: “I would choose Random
    Forest for large datasets, noisy data, mixed feature types, and when
    I want a robust model with minimal preprocessing.”

17. What is Bagging? Answer: “Bagging trains multiple models
    independently on different bootstrap samples and combines their
    predictions. It mainly reduces variance. Random Forest is a classic
    example.”

18. What is Boosting? Answer: “Boosting trains models sequentially. Each
    new model focuses on correcting the mistakes made by the previous
    one. It mainly reduces bias. Examples include AdaBoost, Gradient
    Boosting, and XGBoost.”

19. Difference between Bagging and Boosting? Answer: “Bagging builds
    models independently and reduces variance, whereas Boosting builds
    models sequentially and reduces bias.”

20. Explain Random Forest. Answer: “Random Forest is an ensemble
    learning algorithm that builds multiple decision trees using random
    subsets of data and features. The final prediction is obtained by
    majority voting for classification or averaging for regression.”

21. Explain Logistic Regression. Answer: “Despite its name, Logistic
    Regression is a classification algorithm. It predicts probabilities
    using the sigmoid function and classifies observations based on a
    threshold.”

22. What is Precision? Answer: “Precision tells us, out of all the
    positive predictions made by the model, how many were actually
    correct.”

23. What is Recall? Answer: “Recall tells us, out of all the actual
    positive cases, how many were correctly identified.”

24. What is the F1 Score? Answer: “F1 Score is the harmonic mean of
    Precision and Recall. It is useful when the dataset is imbalanced.”

25. What is Cross Validation? Answer: “Cross Validation evaluates the
    model by splitting the dataset into multiple folds. Every fold is
    used once for validation, and the average performance is reported.”

26. What is Hyperparameter Tuning? Answer: “Hyperparameter tuning is the
    process of finding the best parameter values, such as max_depth or
    n_estimators, to improve model performance.”

27. Tell me about your project. Answer: “I begin by explaining the
    problem statement, then describe the dataset, preprocessing steps,
    feature engineering, algorithms used, evaluation metrics,
    deployment, challenges, and finally the results and lessons
    learned.”

28. Why should we hire you? Answer: “I have built end-to-end machine
    learning projects involving preprocessing, feature engineering,
    model training, evaluation, hyperparameter tuning, Git, Flask
    deployment, Python, SQL, and scikit-learn. Although I am a fresher,
    I learn quickly, enjoy solving problems, and I’m excited to
    contribute while continuously improving my skills.”

29. Why do you want to join our company? Answer: “I want to join your
    company because it offers opportunities to work on real-world
    machine learning problems, collaborate with experienced
    professionals, and continuously grow my technical skills while
    contributing to impactful projects.”

Common Follow-up Questions (Practice)

• Why did you choose Random Forest instead of XGBoost? Suggested Answer:
“I compared multiple algorithms using evaluation metrics and
cross-validation. Random Forest gave the best balance of performance,
stability, and training time for my dataset.”

• How do you handle missing values? Suggested Answer: “I first analyze
the percentage of missing values. Depending on the situation, I remove
rows or columns, or use imputation techniques such as mean, median,
mode, or model-based imputation.”

• How do you know your model is overfitting? Suggested Answer: “If the
training accuracy is significantly higher than the testing accuracy, it
indicates that the model is overfitting.”

• What evaluation metrics do you use? Suggested Answer: “For regression,
I use MAE, MSE, RMSE, MAPE, and R² Score. For classification, I use
Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1 Score, ROC-AUC, and the Confusion
Matrix.”
